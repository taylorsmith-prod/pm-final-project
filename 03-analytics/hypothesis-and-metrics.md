## Step 3 · Craft your hypothesis
- **Qualitative evidence (from M2) , quote the specific friction / moment of misery for your persona:** M2 names the frontline friction as "Too many app navigation layers - 3 taps across 3 screens, every update adds a button, there are menus on menus," and "Route optimization and real-time assessment of closures, traffic, etc. - significant time lost do to inaccurate or delayed routing." The synthesis adds that instability is "forcing drivers and dispatchers onto parallel systems such as paper manifests, screenshots, WhatsApp, and texting." M2 has no coordinator-specific compliance quote, so swap in an interview line about compliance or handoff if you have one.
- **Quantitative evidence (from M3) , name the metric or data point that confirms the pain; cite the number:** Coordinators use the Compliance Checklist heavily (77% adoption) but rate it 2.2 out of 5 (Weak). Logging compliance checks takes 14.6 minutes against a 3.0-minute benchmark (+11.6 min), and completion falls from 71% to 48% at that step. Coordinator NPS fell from +18 to -12, daily time lost to manual workarounds rose from about 9 to 31 minutes (3.4×), and accounts citing complexity as a churn reason rose from 1 of 8 to 4 of 5. Drivers show matching signals: Compliance CSAT of 2.8 and Scheduling CSAT of 1.9.
- **Persona , role, goal, and the friction you confirmed in the reconciliation steps:** The primary persona is a dispatcher or fleet coordinator. Their goal is to assign routes and close out the day's compliance, handoff, and reporting quickly so the fleet keeps moving. The confirmed friction is that after assigning routes, they face a 14.6-minute compliance log, an 11.3-minute handoff, and a 9.7-minute daily report, roughly 30 minutes of overhead a day inside a feature 77% of them use, so they finish the work in spreadsheets and phone calls. Drivers (represented by Diego) are a secondary persona who hit the same kind of friction at the point of action: 3 taps across 3 screens to mark a stop delivered. His workaround of texting the dispatcher pushes work onto the coordinator, so the two problems compound.
- **Problem you are solving , one sentence describing the specific friction this initiative removes:** Coordinators lose about 31 minutes a day to manual workarounds, and drivers add to that load with their own, because RouteLogic's compliance and handoff steps take several times longer than they should and its core actions are buried under extra taps, so both groups finish the job through texts, calls, and spreadsheets instead of inside the tool.
- **Strategic outcome , what behaviour change do you expect, and how does it map to retention / revenue / churn?:** I expect coordinators to complete the post-assignment workflow inside RouteLogic instead of leaving it, which cuts workaround time and rebuilds frontline sentiment. This maps to churn and retention because four of five churned accounts cited complexity, so a faster frontline directly reduces renewal risk at the largest enterprise account. I'm not claiming a revenue figure, since none of the snapshots support one.
- **Primary success metric (initiative signal) , the leading indicator that tells you the gap is closing:** Average daily time lost to manual workarounds, currently 31 minutes. I'd target a 30% reduction, to about 22 minutes. For drivers, I'd add a supporting metric to instrument now: taps and time to mark a stop delivered, currently 3 taps across 3 screens, with a target of one tap. The snapshots have no driver time-lost figure, so that baseline needs to be measured before the pilot, and I'd keep it secondary until it is.
- **Guardrail metric (product signal) , the metric that must NOT drop; it protects your existing base:** Manager Reporting CSAT (currently 4.5) must not fall below 4.0, and Financial Reporting Suite adoption among managers (currently 86%) should hold. Managers are the buyers who chose RouteLogic for its back-office strength, so speeding up the frontline must not erode that. I'd also watch coordinator Core Dispatch CSAT (4.1), since it's the thing they already like.
- **Decision window , how much time or data before you scale, pivot, or kill? minimum threshold to proceed?:** I'd run a 90-day pilot with coordinators at the largest enterprise account. Scale if daily time lost drops 30% or more with the guardrails intact, pivot if the drop is 15 to 29%, and stop below 15%. That leaves roughly nine months of your 12-month window to scale.

The 30% target, one-tap driver target, 4.0 floor, and 90-day window are my proposals rather than figures from your data, so adjust them before submitting.
